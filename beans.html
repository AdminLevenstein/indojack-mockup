Our beans

Sumatra is the big island on the western side of Indonesia, the sixth largest in the world, lying across the equator in the Indian Ocean. Many of its best Arabica coffee beans come from Aceh, where the Gayo highland rises around the town of Takengon and Lake Laut Tawar. Coffee here grows at an average of 1,110 to 1,600 meters, on small family farms.

Arabica first came to the Gayo highland in 1924. Today more than 90% of Indonesia's coffee is grown by smallholders on farms of around one hectare. Gayo farmers process their coffee at the farm using the traditional wet-hulled method, giling basah, which gives the cup its smooth, full body and low acidity. Lintong and Mandheling, two other famous Sumatran coffees grown further south on the island, are made the same way.

The forests are home to a fascinating bird, the hornbill, called Rangkong in Indonesia, living in the mountain rain forests. Close by, Mount Leuser National Park also shelters the Sumatran tiger, the last surviving population on the island, with the rare orangutan and many elephants wandering this beautiful land.
